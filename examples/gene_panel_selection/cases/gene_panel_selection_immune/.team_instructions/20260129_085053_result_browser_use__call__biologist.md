The category mix looks broadly appropriate for tumor–immune profiling: a dominant Signaling/Cancer pathways block (~73%) captures tumor-intrinsic programs and stress responses, while a substantial Lineage/Immune block (~12%) plus Cytokines/Chemokines/Receptors (~5%) and a smaller Antigen presentation/Checkpoints (~1.8%) provide immune identity and effector axes. The main caveat is over-representation of general signaling, which could drown out decision-critical modules (checkpoints, antigen presentation, cytokines) in composite scores and bias pathway enrichment; very small Proliferation and Oncogene/TS/EMT bins may be underpowered for specific calls. For downstream scoring, compute scores at the subcategory level with per-gene normalization (e.g., z-scoring, winsorization), then aggregate using equal weight per subcategory or weights inversely proportional to subcategory size. Practically, upweight Antigen presentation/Checkpoints and Cytokines/Chemokines/Receptors by ~2–3× relative to raw gene counts, keep Lineage/Immune near parity, and cap Signaling/Cancer (and Metabolism/Stress) contributions so they do not exceed ~30–40% of any composite tumor/TME score. Report sentinel-axis scores (e.g., checkpoints, antigen presentation) alongside broader compartment/pathway composites to preserve interpretability.
